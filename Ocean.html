    • Ocean Facts

    Oceans cover about 71% of Earth's surface and contain 97% of the planet's water. They regulate climate, produce oxygen, and support diverse marine life.

    Types of Oceans

    • Pacific Ocean: Largest, covering one-third of Earth's surface.
    • Atlantic Ocean: Second-largest, known for the Gulf Stream.
    • Indian Ocean: Warm waters, home to coral reefs.
    • Arctic Ocean: Smallest, mostly frozen.
    • Southern Ocean: Surrounds Antarctica, rich in biodiversity.

    Ocean Conservation

    Threats include pollution, overfishing, and climate change. Actions like reducing plastic use and supporting marine protected areas help preserve oceans.

    Learn more at NOAA Ocean Education.

    Pacific Ocean

    Largest & Deepest: The largest, covering over 30% of Earth’s surface—more than all land combined.

    Ring of Fire: Known for high seismic activity, containing the deepest place on Earth, the Mariana Trench.

    Geography: Spans between Asia/Australia and the Americas.

    Atlantic Ocean

    Second largest: Covers about 25% of the world's surface.

    Key Features: Home to the Mid-Atlantic Ridge and the notorious Bermuda Triangle.

    Geography: Separates Europe/Africa from the Americas.

    Indian Ocean

    Warmest Ocean: Known as the Warmest, making it rich in marine life coral reefs and turtles.

    Monsoon Driver: Influenced by seasonal monsoon winds affecting its currents.

    Geography: Bounded by Africa, Asia, and Australia.

    Arctic Ocean

    Smallest & Coldest Covers less than 3% of Earth's surface and is largely covered by sea ice.

    Habitat: Essential for polar bears , narwhals, and walruses.

    Climate change: Experiencing rapid, significant reduction in ice due to rising temperatures.

    Southern Ocean

    Newest Named: Officially recognized by many, surrounding Antarctica and formed about 30 million years ago.

    Strongest Currents: Home to the Antarctic Circumpolar Current, the strongest on Earth.

    Characteristics: Known for massive icebergs and cold, nutrient-rich waters.
